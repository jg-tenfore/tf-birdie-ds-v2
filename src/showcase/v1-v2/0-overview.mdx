# V1 → V2 Migration

The rest of the application, brought over from v1 one destination at a time and rebuilt
rather than reproduced.

<SiteLink to="v1-v2" tone="filled" size="md">Open the V1 → V2 tablet prototype</SiteLink>

## What this is

The shipping app's navigation (`tf-birdie-ds-v1`, `references/072926/0-sidebarnav/`) has
nineteen entries: **eighteen destinations** and Log Out, reaching about thirty-four screens.
Before V1 → V2 began, this design system had rebuilt three of the eighteen — the register, the
tee sheet and its settings — and parts of three more: Customer Search, Gift Cards and Events.
The nav already listed all of them; almost everything behind the tiles was missing.

V1 → V2 fills them in. It is a **tablet prototype** of its own, at `/v1-v2/`, alongside Weston
Edits rather than replacing it.

## The rule: keep the jobs, drop the conventions

The instruction for this migration is to audit what v1 *does* — every function a golf course
needs at the counter — and **not** to carry over *how* it does it. v1 documented the shipping
app faithfully, and its own notes are candid about what is wrong with it: a search form whose
three fields read as AND and behave as OR, a validation rule you only discover by failing to
save, a spent gift card signalled only by a dimmed row, two different schedulers for booking a
court and booking a bay.

So every destination here starts from v1's job and v1's warts, and each section opens by saying
which is which: **what v1 did, what was wrong with it, and what this does instead.**

## How it is built

- **On top of Weston Edits.** V1 → V2 is its own edition, `v1v2`, and it inherits everything
  Weston's five rounds settled — the 820 reservation panel, the main nav, notes, the rate
  selector, the customer record. A future Weston round lands once and reaches both prototypes.
  In code, `useWestonEdits()` is true here, and only what is new is gated on `useV1V2()`.
- **The nav keeps v1's groups.** Pro Shop, Restaurant, and the unheaded block — all eighteen
  destinations, in the shipping app's order. What changes is behind each tile.
- **Built in nav order.** Wave 1 finishes the Pro Shop group, wave 2 is the Restaurant, wave 3
  is the rest.
- **Fully working.** Each destination is stateful, deep-linkable and play-tested, to the tee
  sheet's standard — something to use, not only to look at.
- **Tiles go live as they land.** A destination is dimmed in the nav until its screen exists,
  and becomes live by one flag in `pos/data/nav.ts`. The table below reads that same flag.

## Wave 1 · the Pro Shop group

- **Court Sheet** and **Bay Sheet** — one scheduler, configured twice. See sections 3 and 4.
- **The register's missing functions** — combos, holding a ticket, a cash payout and issuing a
  gift card. See section 1.

### Decisions made along the way

Each is the most defensible call without a club to ask, and each is one place to change.

**Courts are paid for, like bays.** v1 never charged for a court: a booking stored a display name
and nothing else. With one model for both sheets, a court needed a price, and the call was to
charge it the way a bay and a tee time are charged — **Check in & pay**, to the register. The
per-court rates are placeholders (tennis $20/hr, pickleball $15, basketball $25, the pool $10)
and are the first thing to correct against a real club. Bays keep v1's own $45/hr.

**Gift cards are not taxed when sold.** A gift card is stored value, not a sale; the tax is
charged when it is *spent*, so taxing the sale would tax the same dollars twice. v1's own model
marked a gift card `taxable: false`. This is the one change to shared pricing in wave 1, and it
only applies to a gift-card line — which only V1 → V2 can create.

**Gift cards expire five years after sale**, the US federal (CARD Act) minimum. v1 defaulted to a
century out and flagged it as needing a decision. Some states forbid expiry altogether, so this
is the setting most likely to need a per-club override.

**Entry limits guard against keypad slips**: a cash payout tops out at $5,000 and a gift card at
$1,000. An extra zero typed into a payout empties a drawer.

**A cash payout is not a sale.** v1 rang it as a negative line inside an order. Here it is a
*drawer event* that never touches the order, recorded so that closing the shift (wave 3) can
account for it.

## Wave 2 · the Restaurant

Six destinations — Quick Order, Tabs, Tables, Reservations, Orders & Tips and Table Chart — and
the largest wave, because v1 had all of them and joined none of them together.

### The chain

The decision everything else follows from: **one connected chain.** A reservation is given a
table; seating it opens a tab on that table, with a seat per guest; the tab's dishes go to the
kitchen; paying the tab closes it, and the table is free. v1's own note on Restaurant Reservations
says "there is nothing here about tables… seating is entirely a matter of the host remembering".

A table's status — free, reserved, seated, waiting on its check, out of service — is **derived**
from the tabs and reservations, never stored. v1 kept a status on each table, which is a second
source of truth: a table can say "seated" with no tab behind it.

### Decisions made for this wave

| | Chosen | Instead of |
|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | One chain: reservation → table → tab → pay → free | v1's three unconnected lists |
| **Kitchen** | **Send and track.** A dish is unsent or sent, and Send fires everything unsent as one ticket. A sent dish's *food* is locked — it changes only by being voided, which the kitchen sees | v1's counter had no kitchen step; its tabs had a per-plate "Fire" buried in a ⋮ menu beside Move, Split and Discount |
| **Menus** | **Counter and 19th Hole**, two menus in one catalog. Any tab sells from either | One combined menu, or the counter alone |
| **Table Chart** | **Full editor**: every shape, barriers, regions, labels, resize and **rotate** handles, undo and redo | A simpler placement editor |

v1's floor model had no rotation; it was asked for with the full editor, so it is an addition
rather than parity.

And three calls made without a question, each one place to change:

- **Modifiers say what they are.** v1's options looked like radio buttons and behaved like
  checkboxes, so staff tapped an option twice expecting the first to clear. Every group here is
  either *one* (a steak's temperature) or *many* (add-ons), and a required group — a burger with no
  temperature — has to be answered before the dish can go to the kitchen.
- **A tab is paid through the register**, the way a tee time is. A dish is a register line with a
  `dish` attached, so the tab's lines arrive on the register exactly as they are.
- **Every payment is recorded.** The store used to remember only the last one, so there was
  nothing for Orders & Tips to show. Checkout now reports the tip apart from the sale, which is what
  makes a tip adjustable at all.

### Caught in review

The first cut of the tab editor offered only what applied to a line — Edit and Remove before it is
sent, Void after — which fixed v1's everything-behind-a-⋮ convention and, in doing so, lost three of
v1's jobs: **Move** a plate to another seat, **Split** one off a line of two, and **Discount** it.
They are back as one **Adjust** control, and it works on sent plates too, because none of the three
touches the food: moving a fired plate from seat 2 to seat 3 changes who pays, not what is cooked.

### Not carried over

- **v1's menu photography.** By v1's own note the dining-room images are Del Frisco's, taken from
  Uber Eats; the counter images have no provenance. Menu tiles are text until there is photography
  that is ours to publish.
- **The cash drop** on Orders & Tips. It is a drawer job, and moves to Shift in wave 3.

### Worth knowing

The order number on a paid reservation (Weston Edits round 5) opened the paid order in the register
until Order Lookup existed. In V1 → V2 it now opens Order Lookup — see wave 3. Weston Edits keeps
the register.

## Wave 3 · everything else

Customer Search, Order Lookup, Time Clock, Gift Cards, Events, Inventory and Shift, plus PIN
sign-in. With this wave every tile in the nav opens a screen. Settings is still the tee sheet's
settings panel, which Weston Edits already had.

The wave is mostly about **money after the sale**: finding an order, giving money back, paying by
something other than a card, and closing the drawer at the end of the day. v1 had screens for all of
it, but none of them shared anything. Order Lookup could find a payment but not what was bought, a
gift card could be sold but not spent, and Shift asked for an ending cash total without saying what
it should be.

### Decisions made for this wave

| | Chosen | Instead of |
|---|---|---|
| **Order Lookup** | **Find, view and refund.** Refund the whole order or chosen lines, back to the tenders that paid | A lookup that only finds orders |
| **Sign-in** | **PIN only.** The prototype opens signed in; Log Out and Switch user bring up the pad | PIN plus manager approval for refunds and voids |
| **House account and card on file** | **Tenders at checkout**, next to the card reader | Settled only from the customer record |
| **Events** | **One event holds golf and spend.** Its golf comes from its tee times; anything charged to it joins a ledger; the organiser is billed once | Separate event and outing records |
| **Inventory** | **Live stock that sales deduct** and refunds return, with counts to correct it | Counts only |
| **Gift cards** | Good for **everything but alcohol** by default, per card | Good for anything |

And the calls made without a question, each in one place to change:

- **A gift card's categories are enforced at checkout.** A card pays for the lines it is good for,
  with their share of the tax, and names the ones it will not pay for. When it cannot cover the order
  it pays part and checkout shows the balance still due. Without this, "not good for alcohol" is
  only a label on a card that pays for the beer anyway.
- **A refund goes back to each tender that paid.** A gift card gets back only what it was good for,
  and the rest goes to the other tenders, most recent first. Refunding a whole order returns exactly
  what each tender paid. The first version sent every refund to the tender that paid the most, which
  put the beers back on a gift card that was never allowed to buy them.
- **Tee times paid before the session refund like any order.** The seeded tee sheet was paid before
  the session opened, so it has order numbers but no orders. Order Lookup builds each one from its
  booking — the same seats, fees and tax — and the refund goes back to a card, because the seed does
  not say how they were paid.
- **Expected cash is worked out, not keyed.** Shift shows the drawer's starting cash, plus cash
  taken, less cash refunds, payouts and drops, all from the day's own payments. v1 asked for an
  ending total and printed a history table so wide its last column was cut off mid-word
  ("End Checl"). Wave 1's payouts and this wave's drops are both drawer events, so they are
  accounted for here.
- **The cash drop is on Shift**, moved from Orders & Tips.
- **After a payment, the next item starts the next order.** The rail keeps showing the paid order
  until **New order**, and a tile tapped in that state used to join the paid order, where nothing
  could pay for it. This fix applies to every edition.

### Left behind from v1

- Membership **baked into the printed name** ("Nadia Marchetti - Full Golf"). It is a chip beside
  the name now, everywhere a name is printed.
- A new-customer rule you **only found by failing to save**. The form now opens by saying it needs
  a last name and a phone number or an email, and ticks each off as it is met.
- Email domain chips that **appended** to what was typed. They replace the domain.
- Order Lookup's **three stacked fields** that read as AND, behaved as OR, and did nothing until
  SEARCH. It is one box, searched as you type.
- A spent gift card shown **only by a dimmed row**. It has a Spent badge.
- Events with **no action bar**, only an overflow menu.

### Not yet

- **Tips on the new tenders.** Gift card, house account, card on file and charge-to-event do not
  take a tip. Checkout keeps the tip on its own screen, and the tender dialogs cannot see it.
- **Printing** shift and stock-count reports.
- **An expected figure for checks.** No tender records checks, so a shift close records the count
  alone.
- **Who may have a house account.** Any customer can be charged; nothing on a customer says they have an account.
- **Low-stock badges in search results.** The register's tiles have them.
