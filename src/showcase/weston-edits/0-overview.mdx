## What Weston asked for

**Golf first, order second.** Today, clicking a tee time treats it like an order: it jumps to
the register, loads charge lines into the cart, and the only golf controls are **+ modifier**
(which is for food and beverage) and **Guest Details**. The order should come *after* the golf.

Clicking a tee time should open the **reservation**. That's where you work the golf:
change the players and the player count, adjust tee fees, switch a player between 9 and 18
holes, open a customer profile, and see ID.me status. Only then does it go to the cart and
checkout. It should open as a **slide-over panel**, so you never lose your place on the tee
sheet. Weston said he could be convinced of a modal instead.

> "It's kind of pre — you're still massaging the reservation before you submit it to the
> cart and then check it out."

| ID.me | **Badge only**, from demo data. The verify flow waits on how Birdie shows it today |
| Bugs found in the recording | **Fixed everywhere**, not only in Weston Edits |
| What **Check in & pay** checks in | **Everyone** on the reservation, except no-shows — on the tablet and the phone |
| A player's fee after switching 9 ↔ 18 | The **rate card**: that hole count's price in the tee time's band (early / peak / twilight), on the player's rate class, or the row's price override. The booked length keeps the booking's own rate. Staff can override; Reset returns to the card |

| Walk-ins | A **reservation too**: Walk-in (or a CHECK IN rate with nothing on the order) books the **next open tee time** after the demo "now", opens it in the panel / reservation screen, and staff can pick another time there. The base edition keeps its fast register-first walk-in |
| Tax | **One calculation, all editions** (`orderTotals`): golf by its booking's tax line, retail and F&B at the sales-tax rate, and the register, checkout, tip, reader and change due all charge that one total |
| Opening a reservation (tablet) | The tee sheet **scrolls it into view** (vertically) beside the panel, grid and list, without moving when it's already visible |
| Demo walk-ins | An `R-` code is a reservation: bookings the seed marked walk-in with one read `booked`. True walk-ins carry a `W-` code |
| Demo prices | **From the rate card**: every priced fixture is the card's price for its hole count, time band and class, so no nine costs more than the eighteen and switching a nine to 18 never lowers the fee. Members and comps stay $0 |
| Demo "now" | **One fixed clock** (`demoNow()`: May 21, 2026, 12:00 PM) for the now-line, the opening scroll, walk-ins, the rate card's default band and the times stamped on payments and activity, so every screenshot is the same whenever it's taken |

## Round 3: the reservation decides the money

A call on **22 September 2026**. Rounds 1 and 2 moved the golf in front of the order; round 3
is about what the counter actually does once it is there. Two things drove it.

The first is that a fee was a **number somebody typed**, and staff do not want a price box:

> "They do want to select what's available, because this is not showing you every rate that you
> have in the system, it's showing you every rate that you could possibly have for **this
> specific tee time on this specific date**."

The second is that a **customer is not part of a tee time**:

> "I don't think it needs to be a tab on the reservation, I wonder if it's its own thing… if I
> click on Michael Thompson, does something else open?"

Neither was expressible in the shape the POS had, where a seat's price was a bare number and a
golfer was five fields. So round 3 started with data.

### The data underneath

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `data/customers.ts` · `customers.json` | The **hundred hand-authored records** ported from v1 — memberships, customer types, punch cards, gift cards, rounds played, no-shows, rewards and balances. Committed, so the database is a thing you can open and read rather than a function of a seed |
| `data/roster.ts` | **340 records**: those hundred, the 28 golfers the POS already priced against, and one per name that can appear on a tee sheet — on any of the eight prototypes. The sheet names are synthesised deterministically *from the name*, so a golfer reads the same way on every run and in every screenshot |
| `data/rate-catalog.ts` | Green fees with **eligibility rules**, the transport catalog, the discount presets, and a 26-rate course for the overflow case |
| `data/carts.ts` | The signout fleet — 35 numbers, skipping 13 the way most fleets do — with availability *derived* from the day's bookings |
| `data/rain-checks.ts` | Credits with balances and redemptions, so a record can say not just that a credit is empty but what emptied it |
| `logic/seat-pricing.ts` | The rate, transport row, discount and punch behind one seat's price, resolved **by reliable key only** |

Synthesising the sheet names rather than re-pointing 200-odd authored bookings at the ported
ones was a deliberate call: re-pointing would have reseeded the demo window that every
screenshot, story and doc depends on, to change strings nobody reads.

**A name is a string until somebody links it.** Only **93 of the 18-hole club's 853** demo
bookings have a booker who resolves to a record — by the booking's phone, the only reliable key
for seat 0 — and that is the rule this round exists to enforce rather than a gap in the
fixtures. A story that wants to show a *record* rather than the assign-a-customer state has to
pick a booking that actually has one (`bookerWithRecord`).

### Decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Where a customer record opens | **Over everything, from the player's name.** A centred modal on the tablet, a full-screen dialog on the phone; closing it leaves the reservation exactly as it was. The reservation drops to **four tabs**, and an old `?res-tab=customer` link degrades to Players rather than breaking |
| What is editable in a record | **Contact details, in place**, with one-tap email domains — Weston's case was "no, actually it's at Gmail", which today means walking all the way to customer lookup. Memberships, punch cards, rain checks and gift cards are shown but **not** edited: taking money stays the register's job, and a second place to do it is a second place for the totals to disagree |
| Customer types | **Chips with an expander** — "show the one you have, and if you want to assign more you can" — not the column of eighteen checkboxes he called ugly |
| An empty seat | Opens the **same surface in assign mode**: search the roster, or create somebody. Linking is what makes that person price the round. A matching name never does |
| What a seat's price is | A **named rate**, not a number. The row says what it is sold on — "Course Level Fee : $26.00" — because a bare dollar amount cannot answer *why is he paying that* |
| Which rates the grid offers | **What this tee time on this date sells**: the catalog narrowed by band, day of week and hole count. The player's record **sorts and groups** the tiles; it never hides one, because the counter's job includes putting somebody on a rate they do not technically qualify for. Ineligible tiles are dimmed and titled *Not this player's rate — staff override* |
| Which rate is picked for you | The **cheapest the player qualifies for**, falling back to the card's rack row when nobody is identified. Picking the rate the system already chose *clears* the choice rather than pinning it, so **Reset** means something |
| A pinned rate across 9 ↔ 18 | It **survives**, and charges that rate's other price. A rate sold for one length only **blocks** the toggle and says why, rather than repricing behind the counter's back |
| Where the rate editor opens | **In place on the row** on the tablet — Weston's own suggestion, "maybe you click and this expands, instead of taking over a full screen" — so the rest of the group never leaves the screen. On the phone it is a **full-screen dialog**: four rows of tiles under an open row on a 402px screen pushes the group off entirely, so you would lose the group *and* have to scroll |
| A grid with too many rows | Past twelve rates it changes shape rather than growing: **Eligible · n** stays visible, the rest collapse behind **Show**, and a filter box runs across both |
| Transport | A **priced catalog**, not a boolean: several carts at different prices, a member cart at nothing, and a **trail fee for walking**. A walker paying $8.58 cannot be expressed by a walk/ride toggle. The icons on the row now select a mode's *default row*, so the toggle and the tiles are two views of one decision |
| Punch cards | A punch card holds prepaid **rounds**, so applying one settles the **green fee** and the ride is **still billed**. The old *Free Punch Cart* transport row is gone — a walker and a rider would have spent the same punch for different value. The punch carries whose card it was, so a member can cover a guest's round, and it is **spent at check-in**, not when applied |
| Discounts | Five presets — comp, 50%, 25%, employee and a typed amount — each carrying a **reason** onto the row and the register line, because a $0 seat with no explanation is what an owner asks about at the end of the month. A punch clears any discount: "50% off a fee already covered by a prepaid round" is not something anybody means |
| Cart signout | Back on the player row as a key glyph. Availability is **derived** from the day's bookings, never stored, so returning a cart is just clearing a seat. A taken cart is **shown struck through with who has it** — "cart 14 is out with the 9:12" is the question actually being asked. Signing a key out implies riding |
| Getting one seat onto the order | Per-seat **Add** — "you hit add to cart for each player, and then you hit save". **Check in & pay** then **tops the order up** to the whole booking rather than rebuilding it, so seats added one at a time keep whatever was done to them |
| Moving between reservations | A **‹ n of m ›** stepper in the panel header, in tee-time order. Empty slots, blocks and events are skipped, the arrows **disable** at the ends rather than wrapping, and a step resets to the **Players** tab |
| Panel width | **640 ships**; 820 and full-cover are a click away in section 10, because Weston asked to feel the difference rather than choose from a description |
| The order rail | **One button, two jobs, never both at once.** With something on the order it is the back arrow and it *clears*, behind a confirm. Once the order is empty it becomes a hamburger and *collapses the rail* to a **56px strip** carrying Walk-in, Reserve and the order count. Anything landing on the order re-expands it |

### What the first pass got wrong

Two corrections worth keeping, because both were believed the other way round first.

**The pricing shipped as a display layer.** A discount, a punch card and a chosen transport row
all changed the player row and nothing else: the row read $0.00 while **Check in & pay** and the
register's **Pay** still charged the full green fee, and the reservation's own "due" figure
disagreed with the row directly above it. Measured on a foursome, applying a punch left the
order at $223.50 — unchanged. `buildTeeTimeCart`, `seatCharges` and `reservationDue` now price
through `seatNetGreenFee`, so the row, the reservation total, the cart line and the tax all
agree with what the counter is about to take; tax follows the net, so a comped seat carries no
green-fee tax. Transport bills the catalog row only once someone picks one, which keeps an
untouched booking pricing exactly as it did before. Five regression tests pin it.

**Dense rows do not fit a foursome in the 640 panel.** This was asserted before it was measured
and it is wrong. The players list stands **823px comfortable and 734px dense** against **618px**
of visible panel — the party stepper and the three group actions take the top of it before a
single seat is drawn. Dense buys back about 90px across a foursome, roughly half a seat; it does
not buy the scroll. Fitting four seats outright is a job for the **820** panel (section 10), not
for the row.

### Variant switches, on the Storybook toolbar

Four questions Weston explicitly declined to answer from a description — "I can be convinced
either way", "I'd want to see it" — so all four ship as switches rather than decisions.

| Toolbar control | Options | The question |
|---|---|---|
| **Panel width** | 640 · ships / 820 · room for rates / covers the sheet | Room to work against how much of the day you can see |
| **Row density** | comfortable / dense | Reading a rate name back to a golfer against seeing more seats at once |
| **Transport** | toggle / named rate | The walk / ride / push icons, or the transport rate it actually bills |
| **Rates** | standard / heavy · 26 | Whether the tile grid holds at a club that prices every band separately. **Story-only** — no prototype ships the heavy catalog |

They wire through `PosProvider`, so every story responds without one of them being edited. A
story that pins a switch itself still wins: the merge order is defaults → toolbar → story, and
section 10 pins the width on the panel so its three screenshots cannot collapse into one. None
of them are linkable — a prototype URL never carries a variant.

### Fixes this round

*All editions:* **clearing the order now confirms** — it is destructive, has no undo, and did it
silently before (the confirm also counts the golf lines, not the tax line, so a threesome no
longer offers to remove "4 items"). And three icon names were never registered — `menu`,
`vpn_key` and `person_search` — so `iconFor` fell back to a neutral bullet and they shipped as
solid dots while the whole suite stayed green. All three are registered, and `icons.test.ts` now
scans every literal icon name in `src` against the registry.

*Weston Edits:* the collapsed rail's Walk-in opened the old dialog while the expanded one booked
the next open tee time as a reservation — the two were written twice and drifted; they now come
from one `QUICK_ACTIONS` list. The base edition's floating "Show order panel" arrow renders only
where there is no strip to carry its own hamburger. Tooltips wrapping disabled buttons never
fired, because a disabled element dispatches no events. The rate editor's headings read
"GREEN FEE · ,9, HOLES", because `String(children)` on interpolated JSX hands back an array;
they are uppercased by CSS now. And `isEligible` required *every* listed customer type while
memberships required any, so a customer carrying plain `Resident` did not qualify for the
Weekday Resident row, which lists two spellings of the same thing.

## How the edition works

An edition is a context, not a fork. Components read `useEdition()` and branch only where
Weston's edits differ, so one component serves both editions and nothing drifts into two
copies. A story picks one with `<Screen edition="weston" />` or `<MobileStory edition="weston" />`.
A prototype build picks one with `VITE_EDITION=weston`. In dev, add `?edition=weston` to the
prototype URL.

When Weston signs off, the edits become the default by changing one line in `edition.tsx`.
