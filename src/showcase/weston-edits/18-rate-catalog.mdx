# 🏷️ Rate Catalog

Sections 1 to 17 are things you can see by clicking. This one is not. The rate catalog is the
model underneath the tile grid — what a rate *is*, who is allowed to buy it, and how the system
decides which one a player is owed — and it is the part that has to be right before any of the
screens mean anything.

Weston's description of what staff expect, from the third call:

> "They do want to select what's available, because this is not showing you every rate that you
> have in the system, it's showing you every rate that you could possibly have for **this
> specific tee time on this specific date**."

That sentence contains the whole model. A rate is not a number in a table; it is a price plus a
rule about when and to whom it may be sold.

## A rate is a price plus a rule

Every rate carries an **eligibility** object. Each field narrows who or when — a field that is
absent does not narrow anything.

| Field | Narrows by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `open` | Nothing. Anyone may buy it | The rack rate |
| `memberships` | Holding **any one** of these memberships | Membership Weekday — *Weekday Golf* |
| `membersOnly` | Holding any membership at all | Member Cart |
| `customerTypes` | Carrying these customer types | Weekday Junior — *Junior* |
| `bands` | Time of day: early, peak or twilight | Junior Twilight |
| `days` | Days of the week, 0 = Sunday | Weekday Resident — Mon to Fri |
| `holes` | Hole counts it is sold for | Premium Single — 18 only |

Two notes on that table, both of which matter at the counter:

- A rate with **no rule** is open to anyone. That is the right default: an unknown rate should
  be offerable, not hidden.
- `customerTypes` and `memberships` both match on **any** of the values listed, not all. The
  standard card's *Weekday Resident* lists two spellings of one idea (`Resident` and
  `Resident 22 Test`), so a customer carrying either qualifies. It read "every" when first
  built, which meant a plain `Resident` did not qualify for the resident rate — the kind of
  fault that is invisible until someone is charged the wrong price at a counter.

## The grid is the slot's rates, not the system's

When the rate editor opens on a player row it does not list the catalog. It lists
**`ratesForTeeTime`** — every rate whose **band**, **day of week** and **hole count** cover this
tee time. Nothing about the player narrows the grid.

That split is deliberate and it is the thing most rate pickers get wrong:

| Question | Who answers it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| *May this rate be sold in this slot at all?* | The slot — band, day, holes | **Filters.** A rate that cannot be sold here is not a tile |
| *Does this player qualify for it?* | The player's record | **Sorts and groups.** It never hides a tile |

Staff can put a player on any tile in the grid, qualified or not, because the counter's job
includes saying "you're getting the member rate today" to somebody who technically isn't. The
tiles a player does not qualify for are dimmed and carry the tooltip *Not this player's rate —
staff override*, which is a record of a decision rather than a warning.

## The system pre-picks, staff decide

For an untouched seat the editor marks the rate the player is **owed**: the cheapest one they
qualify for, falling back to the card's rack row when nobody is identified — an unlinked seat
pays the public rate until someone says otherwise (`autoRate`). Weston: *"it'll automatically
give you what you're supposed to get."*

Choosing a tile pins it to the seat (`PlayerState.rateId`), and three behaviours follow:

- **Picking the rate the system already chose clears the choice** rather than pinning it, so a
  seat only carries a `rateId` when somebody actually overrode something — and **Reset** has a
  meaning.
- **A pinned rate survives a 9 ↔ 18 switch.** Switching holes charges *that rate's* other
  price; it does not move the player to a different rate.
- **A rate that is not sold for the other hole count blocks the switch** instead of repricing
  behind the counter's back. The toggle disables and says which rate is in the way.

Typing a fee over the top still works, and always wins — but the number is no longer the only
thing the row can say, which was the point. A bare `$13.00` cannot answer *why is he paying
that*. The rate's name can.

## The player's own record decides — never the name

Which rate a seat is owed is a question about a **person**, so the seat has to resolve to a
person by a reliable key:

1. a **linked customer record** on the seat (`crmId`), or
2. for the booker, the **booking's phone number**.

**Never the seat's name.** "Kim, D." might be Kim, David or a stranger who shares his surname,
and pricing a round on that guess is the mistake this round of work exists to prevent. A name
that looks like a customer is offered as a *Suggested profile · Link* in the customer record —
it prices nothing until somebody links it, and until then the seat pays the booking's rate.

The consequence runs right through the app: rate class is **per seat**, not per booking. A
member in a guest's foursome plays on the membership row; a guest in a member's foursome pays
rack, with rack's tax and rack's cart fee.

## The standard card

Ten rows, the shape of a real small-club card: three resident rows, four membership rows, an
employee row, a family row and the rack rate that anchors it. Prices are per player, by hole
count, in each band — early (before 10:00 AM), peak (10:00 AM to 2:00 PM) and twilight (from
2:00 PM).

| Rate | Who it is for | Early 18 / 9 | Peak 18 / 9 | Twilight 18 / 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekday Resident | Resident types, Mon–Fri | $46 / $29 | $58 / $36 | $34 / $21 |
| Weekday Senior Resident | Senior, Mon–Fri | $43 / $27 | $53 / $33 | $32 / $19 |
| Weekday Junior | Junior, Mon–Fri | $32 / $17 | $38 / $22 | $22 / $12 |
| Membership Weekday | *Weekday Golf* membership, Mon–Fri | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Membership 7 Days | *Full Golf*, *Corporate — 4 seat*, *30 Day booking window* | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Junior Membership Weekday | *Junior* membership, Mon–Fri | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Employee | Employee type | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Family Full Membership | *Full Golf* membership | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| Family Weekday | *Weekday Golf* or *Trial Month* | $0 | $14 / $8 | $0 |
| **Weekday Non Resident** *(rack)* | Anyone | $54 / $34 | $72 / $46 | $42 / $26 |

The rack row is marked in the data, not guessed by name: it is what an unidentified player is
charged, and what the grid falls back to when nobody qualifies for anything.

Every priced fixture on the demo tee sheet comes from this card, so no nine ever costs more
than the eighteen beside it and switching a nine to 18 can never lower a fee.

## A club with a lot of rates

Weston's other note was that plenty of courses carry far more than ten rows — *"there's some
that have a lot, so we've got to figure out how we handle all of that as well."* So there is a
second, **26-rate** catalog to exercise the grid against: rack prime and rack standard, an
online discount, a Birdie Club rate, resident and senior and junior splits, military, first
responder, employee and employee guest, five membership rows, guest of member, a diamond card,
an 18-only premium single, league, outing and a twilight replay.

It is **story-only** — no prototype ships it. The Storybook toolbar's **Rates** control swaps it
in, and one authored price per rate is scaled through the bands (peak as authored, early at
75%, twilight at 60%) so a rate can never accidentally cost more at twilight than at noon.

Past twelve rates the editor changes shape rather than growing:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Eligible · n** | What this player qualifies for, cheapest first, always visible |
| **All rates · n** | Everything else, behind a **Show** / **Hide** |
| **Filter rates…** | A text filter across both groups |

## Transport is a parallel catalog

Riding is not a boolean, and this is the second half of the model. A course sells several carts
at different prices, a member cart at nothing — and, the case that breaks a walk/ride toggle
outright, **a trail fee for walking**.

| Row | Mode | Price | Who |
|---|---|---|---|
| Riding Cart | Ride | $26.82 | Anyone *(the ride toggle's default)* |
| Cart Plus | Ride | $32.00 | Anyone |
| Member Cart | Ride | $0.00 | Members |
| Walking | Walk | $8.58 | Anyone *(the walk toggle's default — the trail fee)* |
| Walking, member | Walk | $0.00 | Members |
| Push Cart | Push | $6.00 | Anyone *(the push toggle's default)* |

Transport rows answer eligibility with the same `isEligible` the green fees use — one definition
of "qualifies", not two that drift apart. The walk / ride / push icons on the player row now
select a **mode's default row**, and picking a row from the tiles moves the icons with it: the
toggle and the tiles are two views of one decision, and letting them disagree is how a player
ends up riding on a walking fee.

There is deliberately **no punch-card row here** — see section 17. A punch card holds prepaid
rounds, so it settles the green fee and the ride is still billed.

## Discounts carry a reason

The third row of the editor. Weston's case is *"you're the son of a staff guest, let me comp
you"* — one tap, and it has to leave something behind, because a $0 seat with no explanation is
the thing an owner asks about at the end of the month.

| Preset | Takes off | Reason printed on the row |
|---|---|---|
| Comp | The whole fee | Comped |
| 50% off | Half | 50% off |
| 25% off | A quarter | 25% off |
| Employee | The whole fee | Employee |
| Amount… | A figure staff type | Manual discount |

The reason travels with the seat, onto the player row (*"$13.00 · 50% off · was $26.00"*) and
onto the register line. A discount and a punch card are mutually exclusive: applying a punch
clears the discount, because "50% off a fee already covered by a prepaid round" is not something
anybody means.

## What a seat remembers

Everything above is additive, and that is what keeps two hundred authored bookings — and every
test written against them — telling the truth. A seat nobody has touched carries none of these
fields and prices exactly as it did before the catalog existed.

| On `PlayerState` | Set when |
|---|---|
| `rateId` | Staff chose a tile other than the one the system picked |
| `fee` | Staff typed a green fee |
| `transportRateId` / `transportFee` | Staff chose a transport row, or typed its price |
| `discountId` / `discountManual` | A discount preset was applied |
| `punch` | The round was put on a punch card — whose, and which card |
| `holes` | The player was switched off the booking's hole count |
| `cartKey` | A cart was signed out to them |

Nothing here is a snapshot of a price. Every one of them is a *decision*, and the price is
recomputed from the catalog on every render — which is why a seat added to the order one hour
and topped up the next still charges what the reservation says.
